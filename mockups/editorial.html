The Builder who never stopped.

A conversation with Charles Polart, the Paris-based fullstack engineer who has been shipping production software since 2017 — and isn't planning on stopping.

BY T. EDITOR · 1,247 words · PHOTOGRAPHS BY M. ARNAUD · PARIS, 2026

There is something disarmingly simple about the way Charles Polart talks about software. « I ship, » he says, between sips of espresso. « Then I ship some more. »

The first thing Polart will tell you is that he has been writing code since 2017. The second is that he doesn't much care for the meta-discourse around it. The third — and this is where you begin to understand the man — is that he shipped seventeen production projects before he turned thirty, while most of his peers were still arguing about which framework to learn next.

His projects span the modest and the audacious. agora, a community platform he runs out of pure interest. kiwidiag, a diagnostic tool used in independent garages across France. A handful of trading systems that he prefers not to name. « I prefer to ship before I philosophise, » he offers, in what might be the most underrated piece of engineering advice of the decade.

I'd rather ship the boring version that works than the elegant one that doesn't. Charles Polart

His stack is what one might call pragmatic. TypeScript, React, Node, Postgres, with a Rust adventure when the moment calls for it. He laughs when asked about JavaScript fatigue. « Pick the tools that pay the bills, learn the ones that make you think. » He's in the latter mood with Rust at the moment, and currently rebuilding a portion of agora's ingestion layer in it.

What of the future ? Polart is now open to new contracts. He won't pitch you — that is not his style — but he'll mention, in passing, that he is available for engineering roles where, as he puts it, « the work matters and the code doesn't lie. »

« If you'd like to talk, » he says as we wrap up, « just write. » The man, it would seem, has nothing left to prove except, perhaps, what he'll build tomorrow.
